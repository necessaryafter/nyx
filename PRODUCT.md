## Design Context

### Users
Nyx is built first for Brazilian creators who operate dark-content YouTube channels as a production business. They publish narrated videos frequently, care about repeatable output, and want to scale without hiring editors or rebuilding the same video structure by hand.

Their core job is to configure a reusable video template once, then repeatedly turn narration, scripts, assets, and source media into publishable videos with predictable quality and cost. They are comfortable with creator tools and automation, but they are not expected to think like developers.

### Brand Personality
The brand should feel constant, predictable, and reliable. Nyx should communicate quiet operational control rather than excitement. It is a professional production console: direct, clear, steady, and built around confidence in repeated execution.

The product voice should be Brazilian Portuguese by default for the MVP, with concise labels and no exaggerated hype. Copy should help creators understand status, cost, and next actions quickly.

### Aesthetic Direction
The interface is always dark mode. Light mode is not a primary product experience.

The visual direction is cinematic dark with an operational bias: a video-production control room inspired by editing tools and review interfaces, but simpler and more approachable for creator workflows. Use layered dark surfaces, restrained contrast, precise spacing, and rare teal/orange cinematic accents to guide attention.

Avoid crypto-dashboard aesthetics, decorative glow overload, generic SaaS hero patterns, and visual choices that make the product feel unstable or experimental. The interface should feel like a machine that runs every day.

### Design Principles
1. Make repeatability visible: templates, jobs, credits, and render state should all reinforce the promise that the user can configure once and produce consistently.
2. Prefer operational clarity over spectacle: every surface should help the creator decide what to do next, what is happening now, or what will cost credits.
3. Keep dark mode functional: use darkness to reduce fatigue during long editing sessions, with strong contrast and clear focus states.
4. Use motion only for state: animation should clarify loading, saving, rendering, validation, and completion, not decorate the interface.
5. Write in direct Brazilian Portuguese: labels and messages should be short, concrete, and trustworthy.
